1. Pain and Fever Relief (Analgesics/Antipyretics)
These are the most heavily requested over-the-counter (OTC) medicines in any barangay.

Paracetamol (500mg tablet for adults; 125mg/5ml or 250mg/5ml syrup for children; 100mg/ml drops for infants)  

Mefenamic Acid (500mg capsule for pain/inflammation)  

Ibuprofen (or alternative NSAIDs like Celecoxib 200mg for joint pain)

2. Cough, Colds, and Allergies (Respiratory/Antihistamines)
Lagundi (600mg tablet/capsule for adults; 300mg/5ml syrup for kids) – The standard DOH-approved herbal medicine for cough.  

Cetirizine (10mg tablet for adults; 5mg/5ml syrup for kids) – For allergies and runny noses.  

Loratadine (10mg tablet)  

Salbutamol (Nebules 2.5mg or inhalers) – Kept for emergency asthma attacks.  

3. Cardiovascular and Maintenance Drugs (Hypertension)
Barangays regularly distribute these to registered seniors and patients in local hypertension clubs.

Amlodipine (5mg and 10mg tablets)  

Losartan (50mg and 100mg tablets)  

Metoprolol (50mg tablet)

Captopril (25mg tablet) or Clonidine (75mcg tablet) – Strictly kept for hypertensive emergencies.  

4. Diabetes Maintenance (Hypoglycemics)
Metformin (500mg and 800mg tablets) – The primary diabetes drug distributed at the community level.  

5. Gastrointestinal Medicines
Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS) (Sachets) – Crucial for diarrhea, especially in children.  

Aluminum Hydroxide + Magnesium Hydroxide (Chewable tablets or suspension) – For hyperacidity/heartburn.  

Omeprazole (20mg or 40mg capsules)  

6. Vitamins and Supplements
Distributed heavily to pregnant women, infants, and seniors.

Multivitamins (Capsules for adults, syrup for kids)  

Ascorbic Acid (Vitamin C) (500mg tablet; syrup for kids)  

Iron + Folic Acid (Tablets) – Given routinely to pregnant women to prevent anemia.  

Zinc Sulfate (Syrup/Drops) – Given alongside ORS for pediatric diarrhea.  

Vitamin B Complex (Tablets)
